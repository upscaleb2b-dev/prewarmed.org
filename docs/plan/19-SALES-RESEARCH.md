# 19. Outbound practice research

Scoped, per the decision in this session, to **sales practice where it touches deliverability and infrastructure**. That boundary is what keeps the vertical defensible: every page here can be argued from data we hold or from provider mechanics we can cite. General B2B sales advice can be argued by anyone, which is exactly why it would dilute the site.

The test for admitting a page: **does the sending infrastructure behave differently depending on the answer?** If yes, it belongs here. If the answer only affects whether the deal closes, it belongs on somebody else's site.

## Out of scope, explicitly

Discovery calls, objection handling, pricing conversations, pipeline management, quota design, CRM hygiene, sales hiring, AI SDR tooling as a category. Each is a reasonable topic and none of them changes what a mail server does.

S12 is the boundary page and it is written carefully: it diagnoses whether a problem is infrastructure, list or message, and when the answer is "your message is bad" it says so and stops, rather than continuing into copywriting advice.

## Why this vertical earns its place

Two reasons beyond traffic. It is the bridge between the research and the reader's actual job, which makes the research feel applicable rather than academic. And it is where the operator voice is most obviously credible: a team that sends a million emails a month has watched cadence changes move complaint rates, and almost nobody else can write that sentence with numbers attached.
