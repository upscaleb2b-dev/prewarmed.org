The new standard for deliverability

Prewarmed means 51 days of reputation built before your first send.

Thirty days aged, so the domain has a registration history and authentication that has been published long enough to be believed. Then twenty-one days warmed, sending and receiving real mail so the receiving providers have formed an opinion of it. Most of what is sold as prewarmed skips one of those, or both.

The 51 daysWhat happens

There is a science to this, and most of it is waiting correctly

Neither half can be compressed. Age cannot be manufactured on the day you need it, and engagement history cannot be built faster than a receiving provider is willing to observe it.

Aging

days 0 to 30 · no sending
  • Register a clean .com, checked against drop history and prior blocklist exposure
  • Publish SPF, DKIM and DMARC, and leave them published

That is the whole job. Nothing sends for thirty days. The domain is accruing the one thing that cannot be bought on the day you need it.

Warming

days 30 to 51 · real traffic
  • Mailboxes created on Google Workspace, Microsoft 365 or Azure
  • Low-volume sending and receiving, ramped in steps rather than on a calendar
  • Real engagement: opens, replies, and messages moved out of spam
  • Placement tested before handover, not after
  • Anything failing is fixed and retested, or the domain does not ship

Why thirty days and not seven. A domain that starts sending in its first days gets listed on the major blacklists almost immediately, and an early listing is the hardest kind to undo. Registration age is one of the very few signals a receiving provider can check that a sender cannot fake or accelerate, and it is the cheapest protection available against exactly that. It is also the step most sellers skip, because waiting costs them inventory turnover.

How this site works

Named, and reviewed

Every page carries an author and a reviewer who did not write it. For research, the reviewer re-runs the analysis from the committed script before sign-off.

Dated and re-verified

Published date, last-verified date and the verification interval sit at the top of every page. A stale page fails our build rather than going quietly out of date.

Corrections in public

Confirmed errors are fixed within five working days and logged with what was wrong and what changed. A research site with no corrections is one nobody is checking.

Free and ungated

No account, no email capture, no gated downloads. Charts and aggregated data are published under CC BY 4.0 so you can redraw them and check the arithmetic.
